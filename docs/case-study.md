# Case study: human-centered field-service modernization

## Context

A pest-control organization is considering a connected branch-office and tablet-based field-management system. The proposed change could replace paper-heavy workflows with shared scheduling, treatment history, customer updates, and field access to operational information.

The central question is not whether the technology is new. It is whether the system improves real work for the people who depend on it every day.

## Stakeholders and needs

| Stakeholder | Desired outcome | Risk to manage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Field technicians | Fast, reliable access to schedules, records, and treatment guidance | Poor connectivity, extra data entry, or a workflow designed without field input |
| Office staff | Fewer duplicate records and better visibility into job status | Incomplete data or unclear ownership during transition |
| Customers | Timely updates and consistent service history | Digital access barriers and loss of trust if communication becomes impersonal |
| Management | Better coordination, recordkeeping, and operational insight | Treating software purchase as the solution without adoption planning |

## Analysis

The system has potential to improve coordination, reduce paperwork, and make information available closer to the point of service. Those benefits depend on usability, training, support, and feedback from technicians before full rollout.

Digital inclusion is a material implementation concern. Rural workers and customers may have inconsistent broadband, different levels of device access, or limited comfort with new interfaces. A successful rollout therefore needs offline-tolerant workflows, accessible support, and non-digital alternatives during the transition.

The analysis also identifies a governance concern: operational records may include customer, treatment, and regulatory information. Access controls, retention practices, auditability, and clear recordkeeping responsibilities should be designed alongside the user experience.

## Recommendation

Use a staged, human-centered rollout:

1. Map current field and office workflows with representative users.
2. Prototype the highest-value technician and dispatcher tasks.
3. Pilot with a small group across different connectivity and experience levels.
4. Measure task completion, rework, support requests, adoption, and customer impact.
5. Improve the workflow before expanding deployment.

## Success measures

- Fewer duplicate or missing job records
- Faster access to current schedules and treatment history
- Lower paper and phone-based coordination overhead
- High completion rates for core technician tasks
- Decreasing support requests after training
- Positive technician and customer feedback
- Auditable handling of operational records

## Takeaway

Technology modernization is a socio-technical change. The strongest solution is the one that respects field realities, includes the people who use it, and improves service without creating a new accessibility or trust problem.
