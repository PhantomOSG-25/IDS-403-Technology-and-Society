# Analysis framework

## Lenses used

### Social-science lens

Examines how the system changes workplace relationships, job expectations, customer communication, access, and adoption.

### Human-centered design

Treats technicians, office staff, and customers as participants in discovery, prototyping, testing, and continuous improvement.

### Responsible-technology lens

Asks who benefits, who may be excluded, what information is collected, how it is protected, and how the organization can remain accountable.

## Decision questions

- Does the proposed workflow solve a verified operational problem?
- Can a technician complete the task under real field conditions?
- What happens when connectivity, device access, or user confidence is limited?
- How will feedback become measurable product improvements?
- What records require access controls, retention rules, or audit trails?

## Risks and mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation |
| --- | --- |
| Workflow does not fit field conditions | Observe real work and pilot with technicians |
| Uneven device or broadband access | Offline support, training, and transitional alternatives |
| Low adoption | Role-based training, local champions, and feedback loops |
| Inaccurate or duplicated records | Validation, clear ownership, and reconciliation checks |
| Exposure of customer or treatment data | Least-privilege access, retention rules, and audit logs |
